    Wedge refusal¶

    Refusal policies: declining a trijunction observation the mesh's own geometry makes infeasible.

    A WedgeRefusal is passed as refusal= to infer_tensions and its relatives, and to infer_tensions_young_dupre_local. Only the methods of foambryo.tension_inference.ANGLE_READING_METHODS honour it; the others raise ValueError on any policy but NO_REFUSAL. refusal_report counts what a policy declines.

    The problem¶

    At a trijunctional edge three films meet and the three interior wedge angles sum to a full turn. A reflex wedge is one wider than a straight angle: the surface is locally folded there, the three films do not enclose the edge the way a trijunction requires, and the Young-Dupré balance every angle-reading estimator in this package imposes has no solution for such a triple. Feeding the estimator that row is feeding it an observation that cannot have come from the object being measured.

    Two facts make this worth acting on rather than documenting:

    • foambryo.geometry's oriented reading can see the fold — but it cannot undo it. The per-edge estimator's rows are built from cos of the wedge, and cos is even about a full turn, so a reflex wedge and its reflection are literally the same row; no reading reaches it.
    • The log-of-sin estimator does not merely degrade on such a row, it fails: a reflex wedge has a negative oriented sine, and the row's logarithm is not defined.

    So the estimator should refuse the observation. This module is the machinery for that, and it is off by default — NO_REFUSAL reproduces every existing number bitwise, and which policy (if any) should become a default is a separate decision taken on its own evidence.

    The three policies, and the unit each acts on¶

    Every angle-reading estimator here builds its rows from one of two observation units: a whole junction line (the region triple), for the methods that average the wedge angles along the line and weight the row by the line's arclength share; or a single trijunctional edge, for the per-edge method, whose row is one edge. The policies are defined on the unit, so each is one intervention with one meaning in both families:

    • :attr:WedgeRefusalPolicy.ExcludeEdgeFromLineMean (E) — a refused edge contributes neither its angles nor its length to its line. The line keeps its row, computed from the surviving edges and weighted by their surviving arclength. A line that loses every edge has no mean and therefore no row.
    • :attr:WedgeRefusalPolicy.DropLine (D) — a line carrying at least one refused edge loses its row entirely.
    • :attr:WedgeRefusalPolicy.DownweightLine (W) — nothing is removed from the measurement: the line's angle means are what they always were, and only its row weight is scaled, by the fraction of its arclength that is not refused. A line with no unrefused arclength has weight zero, which is the same as having no row.

    A refused wedge takes the other two wedges of its own edge with it. The three are one observation of one edge and every row consumes all three; dropping one wedge key and keeping its two partners would leave a line's three means computed over different edge subsets and no longer summing to a full turn.

    Two consequences follow from the definitions and are asserted as tests rather than assumed (tests/test_wedge_refusal.py):

    1. For the per-edge method the unit is one edge, so under E a refused edge's unit is empty and it has no row — E and D are the same intervention there and agree exactly. W is not: it keeps the refused rows and scales every row of their line instead.
    2. For the line-averaged methods E and W give a surviving line the same row weight (E's surviving arclength over the surviving total; W's full arclength times the surviving fraction, over that same total). So E and W differ only in the angle a line reports, and D and E only in whether a partly folded line reports at all.

    Underdetermination is an outcome, not an error¶

    Refusing rows can leave an interface with no surviving junction line at all, or split the junction graph into pieces that no longer share a gauge. That is handled exactly as foambryo.junction_graph already handles a closed interface and a fragmented mesh: the partition is rebuilt over the surviving lines (junction_graph.partition_over_surviving_junctions), an interface touched by no surviving line is reported float("nan") and never a number, a survivor graph in several pieces is normalised in each piece, and JunctionGraphGaugeWarning fires unchanged. A mesh all of whose lines are refused returns every interface undetermined rather than raising: an empty answer is a result, and it is one the caller can count.

    Report the refused counts next to any accuracy number. A policy that improves an error statistic by declining a large share of the data has not improved anything, and :func:refusal_report is what makes that visible.

    Two reasons to decline an edge: a fold, and the crop¶

    A junction edge with a vertex on a crop face (foambryo.crop_faces) is not an observation of the tissue either: the tissue beyond the crop was cut away. It is declined for that reason on every cropped mesh, whatever the policy, and always edge by edge: it leaves its line's mean and length, and the rest of the line still reports. The policy then acts on what remains, as if the crop edges were not part of the line: under DropLine a line is dropped when one of its other edges is refused, and under DownweightLine its weight is the unrefused fraction of the arclength that is not on the crop. The two sets are held apart (WedgeRefusal.crop_edges beside the policy's own edges) and counted apart in RefusalReport. On a mesh without crop faces the crop set is empty and nothing here changes.

    Reading nothing but the mesh¶

    :func:reflex_trijunction_edges takes a mesh and a tolerance. There is no parameter through which a reference geometry, a target angle or an expected value could reach it, and the decision is therefore available on data that has no reference at all.

    WedgeRefusalPolicy ¶

    Bases: Enum

    Which observations an angle-reading estimator declines, and how. See the module docstring.

    • NoRefusal: the default. Nothing is declined and every result is bitwise what it was before this module existed.
    • ExcludeEdgeFromLineMean: a refused edge leaves its line's angle mean and its line's length; the line still reports, from what survives.
    • DropLine: a line carrying any refused edge does not report at all.
    • DownweightLine: every line reports its unchanged mean, with its row weight scaled by the fraction of its arclength that is not refused.

    per_edge_rows ¶

    per_edge_rows(mesh: DcelData, trijunction_edges: Sequence[TrijunctionEdge], refusal: WedgeRefusal = NO_REFUSAL) -> PerEdgeRows

    Apply a refusal policy to a per-edge estimator, whose observation unit is one edge.

    ExcludeEdgeFromLineMean and DropLine coincide here and the coincidence is exact, not approximate: the unit is a single edge, so excluding a folded edge from its own unit's mean empties that unit, and an empty unit has no row — which is what dropping it means. They are the same intervention. DownweightLine is genuinely different: it keeps every row, including the folded ones, and scales the whole line's rows by its unrefused arclength fraction.

    mesh DcelData

    Mesh to analyze — read only to decide which edges are folded.

    		 required
    trijunction_edges Sequence[TrijunctionEdge]

    the estimator's own edge list, under whichever reading it uses. The edge set and lengths do not depend on the reading.

    		 required
    refusal WedgeRefusal

    which observations to decline. Defaults to refusing nothing, in which case every edge is kept with scale 1.0.

    		 NO_REFUSAL

    Returns:

    Name Type Description
    PerEdgeRows PerEdgeRows

    the surviving rows and their weight scales.
